Format guides

Each guide covers one format: what the file is, how it differs from its neighbours, and how to open it on Android or iPhone with Database Viewer.

MDB viewer

Legacy Microsoft Access databases from the Jet era (Access 97 through 2003), opened on the phone without Microsoft Access.

ACCDB viewer

Access 2007 and later — the ACE format — read on-device, with attachment and multi-value fields in place.

MDB to CSV

Export an Access table as CSV, or bundle the whole database as one XLSX workbook with a sheet per table.

DBF viewer

dBASE tables and shapefile attribute data, readable without dBASE, FoxPro or Excel — encoding checked up front.

SQLite viewer

Open .sqlite, .sqlite3 and .db files, inspect the schema, follow foreign keys and run SQL queries on the phone.

ODS viewer

OpenDocument spreadsheets — the open standard behind LibreOffice and OpenOffice — opened without LibreOffice or Excel.

Frequently asked questions

What formats does the app support?

Database Viewer opens ten formats on Android and iPhone: MDB and ACCDB (Microsoft Access), SQLite, DBF (dBase), ODS (OpenDocument spreadsheets), XLSX and XLSM (Excel workbooks), and CSV, TSV and SQL dump as plain-text data. Each guide on this page covers one of them in detail.

Is it free?

Free to start: the app is ad-supported, and opening files, reading the health report and browsing tables with a generous free preview all work without paying. Pro — a single one-time purchase — removes the ads and adds export, editing and more.

Is my data uploaded?

No. Every file is read on the phone itself: nothing is sent to a server, no account is required, and the original file opens read-only.

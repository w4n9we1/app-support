Convert winmail.dat to PDF on iPhone

“winmail.dat to PDF” means turning the TNEF message Outlook sent into a readable PDF. On iPhone or iPad, Winmail Reader decodes the file on-device and exports the whole email — sender, recipients, date, subject, body and the attachment list — to a PDF you can share; nothing is uploaded.

What does the exported PDF actually contain?

It contains the message, laid out as a clean document: the header fields (sender, recipients, date, subject), the body rendered from whichever version the file carries — HTML, RTF or plain text — and a table listing every attachment with its file name and size.

The attachments themselves are not baked into the PDF. They are extracted as the original files they were sent as, so a PDF someone emailed you stays a separate, usable PDF and a Word document is not flattened into pages. If your goal is the files rather than the message, opening the winmail.dat and using the attachment actions is the shorter route.

How to convert a winmail.dat file to PDF on iPhone or iPad

  1. Install Winmail Reader from the App Store (iOS 17.0+ / iPadOS 17.0+). It is free to look inside.
  2. Open the winmail.dat file. In Mail, long-press the attachment and choose Save to Files, or tap Share and pick Winmail Reader. Files arriving through Files, iCloud Drive or another app work the same way.
  3. Check what the file holds. The app decodes the TNEF package on-device and shows the sender, subject and first screen of the message, plus the attachment list. The file check-up explains the detection if the file is unusual or partly damaged.
  4. Export the email to PDF. With Pro, open the message, choose PDF in the export row, and the decoded email is rendered to a PDF saved inside the app — from there, share it or save it to Files.

A single message can also be exported to EML, HTML, TXT or RTF. PDF is the fixed, printable choice; EML is the one that stays re-importable in another mail app.

Online converter or on-device app?

An online winmail.dat to PDF converter has to receive the whole file before it can return anything. Mail attachments routinely hold invoices, contracts and client documents, so that upload is the part worth thinking about. Both approaches produce a PDF — the difference is where the file is decoded.

Online converter

The file leaves your device before you see any output. Processing happens on a third-party server, and its retention policy is outside your control.

Winmail Reader — on-device

Decoding and PDF rendering run on your iPhone or iPad. Nothing is uploaded, no account is required, and the original file stays read-only. The exported PDF stays in the app's folder until you share or delete it.

Frequently asked questions

Can I convert a winmail.dat file to PDF on iPhone?

Yes. Winmail Reader can export a decoded winmail.dat message as a PDF on iPhone or iPad, with no Windows PC and no Outlook involved. The file is decoded on your device first, and the export itself is one of the Pro actions.

Does the PDF include the attachments?

No. The PDF contains the message — sender, recipients, date, subject, body and a table listing each attachment with its file name and size. The attachments themselves are extracted as separate original files, not merged into the PDF, so a Word file stays a Word file.

Is converting winmail.dat to PDF free?

No. Free to look inside: open the file and see the sender, subject and first screen of the message, the attachment list, and the file check-up. Reading the full message and exporting it to PDF are part of Pro — a single one-time purchase. An optional monthly subscription is also available, priced as shown in the app.

Are my files uploaded when converting?

No. Decoding and PDF rendering both happen on your iPhone or iPad: no account and no uploads. Your original winmail.dat is opened read-only, and the exported PDF stays in the app's own folder until you share it or delete it.

Which formats can I export an email to?

A single decoded message can be exported to PDF, EML, HTML, TXT or RTF. PDF is the one to pick when you need a fixed, printable document; EML keeps the message re-importable in another mail app. XPS documents have their own XPS-to-PDF export.

Will the PDF look exactly like the original email?

It reproduces the header fields, the message body and the attachment table in a clean, fixed layout built for reading and printing. Styling is normalised rather than pixel-copied, so a heavily designed HTML newsletter may look plainer in the PDF than it did in Outlook.

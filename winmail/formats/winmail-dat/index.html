What is a winmail.dat file?

winmail.dat is what Microsoft Outlook produces when it sends a message in TNEF — Transport-Neutral Encapsulation Format. Instead of sending a normal MIME message, Outlook packs the body, the real attachments and any calendar invitations into one proprietary container and names it winmail.dat (sometimes ATT00001.dat). Apple Mail, Gmail and most other clients cannot read that container, so all you see is one attachment you cannot open.

Inside, nothing is lost: the message text, the attached PDFs, Word and Excel files, images, calendar invites and contact cards are all still there. A TNEF decoder simply has to unpack them. Winmail Reader does that with a purpose-built decoder that runs on your iPhone or iPad, so the file never has to leave your device.

Why did you get winmail.dat instead of attachments?

The cause is on the sender's side. Outlook can be configured to send mail to external recipients in Rich Text Format; Exchange then wraps everything in TNEF. The sender can prevent it by choosing HTML or plain text for external recipients in Outlook's options, or by asking their administrator. On the receiving side, opening the winmail.dat file restores the original attachments — you do not need Outlook, Exchange or a Windows PC.

How to open winmail.dat on iPhone or iPad

  1. Install Winmail Reader from the App Store (iOS 17.0+ / iPadOS 17.0+). It is free to look inside.
  2. Save or share the file. In Mail, long-press the winmail.dat attachment and choose Save to Files, or tap Share and pick Winmail Reader. Files arriving through Files, iCloud Drive or another app work the same way.
  3. Read the decoded message. The app shows the sender, subject and first screen of the message, plus the complete attachment list. A check-up explains what the file really is if detection is uncertain.
  4. Unlock the rest with Pro. Full message reading, attachment preview and extraction, PDF/EML export and in-mail search are part of the one-time Pro purchase. An optional monthly subscription is also available, priced in the app.

An online winmail.dat converter cannot show you anything until the whole file has been uploaded to its server. That copy is outside your control — and mail attachments often contain contracts, invoices, passport scans or client data.
